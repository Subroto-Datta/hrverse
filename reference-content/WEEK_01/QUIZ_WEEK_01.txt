Q1. Which perspective views human capital not as an operational expenditure to be minimized, but as a primary competitive advantage?
A) Classical Scientific Management
B) Strategic Human Resource Management (SHRM)
C) Bureaucratic Organization Theory
D) Industrial Taylorism
Correct: B
Explanation: Strategic Human Resource Management (SHRM) links people management practices directly with long-term strategic enterprise objectives, viewing human capital as a core value driver.

Q2. What is the fundamental distinction between a Job Description and a Job Specification?
A) Job descriptions detail salary bands; job specifications define reporting hierarchies.
B) Job descriptions define tasks, duties, and responsibilities (TDRs); job specifications define Knowledge, Skills, Abilities, and Other characteristics (KSAOs).
C) Job descriptions are public; job specifications are internal secrets.
D) Job descriptions are updated annually; job specifications never change.
Correct: B
Explanation: Job descriptions capture the observable work and responsibilities (TDRs), while job specifications state the human competencies and credentials required (KSAOs).

Q3. In quantitative workforce planning, which technique establishes statistical relationships between organizational volume (e.g., sales revenue) and required headcount?
A) Delphi Method
B) Trend / Ratio Regression Analysis
C) Nominal Group Technique
D) Succession Scenario Planning
Correct: B
Explanation: Ratio analysis and regression modeling correlate operational volume indicators (e.g., units produced, revenue) with staffing requirements.

Q4. What is the main threat to validity when conducting an unstructured interview?
A) Overly rigid scoring criteria
B) Cognitive bias, halo/horns effects, and inconsistent questioning across candidates
C) Excessive predictive accuracy
D) High statistical reliability
Correct: B
Explanation: Unstructured interviews lack standard questions and evaluation rubrics, making them highly susceptible to interviewer bias, intuition errors, and poor predictive validity.

Q5. Which selection tool consistently exhibits the highest predictive validity in employment testing literature?
A) Standalone Myers-Briggs personality indicator
B) Work sample tests and structured behavioral interviews
C) Reference checks from past colleagues
D) Analysis of personal handwriting (graphology)
Correct: B
Explanation: Schmidt & Hunter meta-analyses demonstrate that work sample tests (~0.54) and structured behavioral interviews (~0.51) offer the highest predictive validities for job performance.

Q6. In the STAR interview technique, what does the acronym represent?
A) Strategy, Timing, Action, Reward
B) Situation, Task, Action, Result
C) Standards, Technique, Attitude, Reliability
D) System, Target, Alignment, Review
Correct: B
Explanation: STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result—a structured framework for eliciting and evaluating behavioral examples.

Q7. What does the "4/5ths Rule" (or 80% Rule) measure in employment selection?
A) Whether a recruitment test takes less than 80 minutes to finish
B) Potential adverse impact against a protected class in hiring selection ratios
C) The percentage of employees who pass annual probation
D) The required offer acceptance rate for campus hiring
Correct: B
Explanation: Under EEOC guidelines, if the selection rate for a protected group is less than four-fifths (80%) of the rate for the group with the highest rate, adverse impact is indicated.

Q8. Which talent metric measures the total duration from job opening requisition approval to the day the final candidate accepts the written offer?
A) Time to Productivity
B) Time to Fill
C) Time to Interview
D) Sourcing Cycle Speed
Correct: B
Explanation: Time to Fill calculates the total calendar days from formal requisition approval until candidate offer acceptance.

Q9. In the Delphi technique for HR demand forecasting, what is the key characteristic of the panel?
A) Experts meet face-to-face and debate loudly until consensus
B) Independent, iterative anonymous questionnaires with aggregated feedback
C) Algorithmic machine learning models replace human opinions entirely
D) Decisions are made solely by the Chief Human Resources Officer
Correct: B
Explanation: The Delphi method relies on an iterative, anonymous survey of subject-matter experts to eliminate peer pressure and groupthink.

Q10. What is an internal talent marketplace?
A) A public website where contractors sell services
B) A platform allowing existing employees to discover gigs, project assignments, and transfers across the enterprise
C) An auction platform for employee overtime shifts
D) A procurement software for hiring temporary agency staff
Correct: B
Explanation: An internal talent marketplace matches existing organizational talent with internal opportunities, cross-functional projects, and role transitions.

Q11. Which document establishes the foundational rights of workers globally through international labor standards?
A) The Sarbanes-Oxley Act
B) The International Labour Organization (ILO) Conventions
C) The Dodd-Frank Declaration
D) The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
Correct: B
Explanation: The ILO (a specialized agency of the UN) develops international labor conventions addressing fundamental rights at work.

Q12. What is the "Silver Medalist" candidate strategy in Talent Acquisition?
A) Offering second-tier compensation packages to entry-level hires
B) Proactively nurturing and reconsidering highly rated runner-up candidates from previous searches
C) Hiring competitors' second-in-command executives only
D) Rewarding hiring managers who finish recruiting under budget
Correct: B
Explanation: Silver medalists are vetted, qualified finalists who were not selected for a specific role but remain exceptional prospects for future openings.

Q13. In workforce succession planning, what is the primary purpose of a 9-Box Grid?
A) Allocating workspace seating arrangements
B) Evaluating employees simultaneously on Performance (past delivery) and Potential (future growth capacity)
C) Tracking weekly hours worked across nine job grades
D) Designing nine tiers of healthcare benefits
Correct: B
Explanation: The 9-Box Grid plots employees on two axes: current performance and future leadership/growth potential.

Q14. How is Cost per Hire (CPH) calculated according to ISO and SHRM standards?
A) Base Salary of Hire divided by 12 months
B) (Sum of Internal Recruiting Costs + Sum of External Recruiting Costs) divided by Total Number of Hires
C) Total HR Department Budget divided by Total Company Headcount
D) Agency commission percentage multiplied by gross revenue
Correct: B
Explanation: CPH = (Internal Recruiting Costs + External Recruiting Costs) / Total Hires made within the period.

Q15. In modern structured onboarding, what is the primary focus of the first 30 days?
A) Executing multi-million dollar strategy decisions without supervision
B) Cultural immersion, role expectations alignment, compliance, and foundational tool access
C) Conducting annual performance reviews
D) Taking over organizational restructuring
Correct: B
Explanation: The first 30 days are designed for orientation, psychological safety, understanding team context, and mastering tools.

Q16. What is the "Halo Effect" in candidate evaluation?
A) Scoring a candidate poorly due to a single perceived flaw
B) Allowing one positive candidate trait (e.g., charisma, alma mater) to positively bias judgment across all competencies
C) Systematically hiring only candidates who attended religious universities
D) Giving every candidate the exact same high score
Correct: B
Explanation: The Halo Effect occurs when one striking positive characteristic blinds the evaluator to other weaknesses.

Q17. Which sourcing channel typically demonstrates the highest 1-year retention and fastest time-to-hire?
A) Blind cold postal outreach
B) Employee Referral Programs
C) Generalist internet pop-up ads
D) Unverified resume scraper databases
Correct: B
Explanation: Employee referrals consistently deliver higher cultural alignment, faster onboarding, and significantly higher 1-year retention rates.

Q18. What is the fundamental goal of Job Enrichment according to Herzberg's Motivator-Hygiene Theory?
A) Adding more mundane, repetitive tasks to a worker's shift (Job Enlargement)
B) Increasing autonomy, responsibility, depth, and opportunities for personal achievement in the job role
C) Installing air conditioning and clean water fountains
D) Eliminating all performance reviews
Correct: B
Explanation: Job Enrichment involves vertical loading of a role, granting greater discretion, responsibility, and cognitive engagement.

Q19. What does the term "Offer Acceptance Rate" (OAR) gauge?
A) The percentage of applied candidates who receive job offers
B) The percentage of candidates extended a formal written offer who sign and accept
C) The percentage of new hires who pass probation
D) The ratio of job applicants to open requisitions
Correct: B
Explanation: OAR = (Accepted Job Offers / Total Offers Extended) * 100%, indicating competitiveness of offers and brand appeal.

Q20. What is an Assessment Center in modern employee selection?
A) A physical medical clinic for health checks
B) A standardized multi-exercise evaluation featuring role-plays, in-basket exercises, and simulations evaluated by multiple trained observers
C) An online typing speed test platform
D) A payroll processing office
Correct: B
Explanation: An Assessment Center utilizes standardized behavioral simulations, presentations, and group exercises scored by calibrated assessors.
