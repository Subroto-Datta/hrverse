TOPIC 1: UNDERSTANDING THE HR DEPARTMENT
1.1 What is an HR Department?
An HR department is a functional unit within an organisation responsible for managing employee-related activities throughout the employment lifecycle.

It ensures that the organisation recruits suitable employees, maintains employee records, manages workplace relationships, coordinates compensation processes and supports employee development.

The HR department acts as a bridge between employees and management.

It must balance two important interests:

Organisational requirements: Productivity, performance, cost management and business objectives.
Employee requirements: Fair treatment, compensation, development, workplace safety and professional growth.
1.2 Why Do Organisations Need an HR Department?
Consider a company with 500 employees.

Without a dedicated HR function, the organisation may face problems such as:

Unstructured recruitment.
Incorrect employee records.
Delayed onboarding.
Attendance discrepancies.
Payroll coordination problems.
Employee dissatisfaction.
Workplace conflicts.
Lack of performance evaluation.
Compliance failures.
An HR department establishes structured processes to prevent and address these problems.

1.3 Core Responsibilities of an HR Department
The following are the major responsibilities:

Responsibility	What HR Does
Workforce Planning	Identifies employee requirements
Recruitment	Finds and hires suitable candidates
Onboarding	Coordinates new employee joining
Employee Records	Maintains employee information
Payroll Coordination	Coordinates salary processing
Employee Relations	Handles grievances and workplace concerns
Performance Management	Coordinates performance evaluation
Learning & Development	Organises employee training
Compliance	Supports applicable employment-law compliance
Employee Engagement	Develops initiatives to improve employee experience
Separation	Coordinates employee exit procedures
Important: In smaller organisations, one HR professional may handle several of these responsibilities. In larger organisations, separate teams may manage each function.

TOPIC 2: HR DEPARTMENT HIERARCHY AND DESIGNATIONS
An HR department can have multiple levels of responsibility depending on the size and structure of the organisation.

Let's understand the common hierarchy.

2.1 Chief Human Resources Officer (CHRO)
The CHRO is generally the highest-ranking HR executive in a large organisation.

The CHRO is responsible for aligning the organisation's human resource strategy with its overall business strategy.

Major Responsibilities:

Developing long-term HR strategies.
Advising the CEO and senior leadership.
Workforce planning at an organisational level.
Leadership development.
Succession planning.
Organisational culture.
Talent management.
HR policy direction.
Monitoring overall workforce performance.
Example:

A company plans to expand from 500 to 2,000 employees over three years.

The CHRO works with senior management to develop a workforce strategy covering recruitment, leadership requirements, employee development and HR budgets.

Skills Required:

Strategic thinking.
Leadership.
Business understanding.
Decision-making.
Communication.
Workforce planning.
2.2 HR Director
An HR Director leads the HR function or a major HR division.

The position may report directly to the CHRO or another senior executive, depending on the organisation.

Major Responsibilities:

Implementing HR strategy.
Supervising HR managers.
Establishing HR policies.
Monitoring HR department performance.
Managing HR budgets.
Ensuring consistent HR practices.
Coordinating with other departments.
Example:

An HR Director supervises recruitment, HR operations and employee development teams across multiple company locations.

2.3 HR Manager
An HR Manager manages the day-to-day functioning of the HR department or a specific HR team.

Major Responsibilities:

Supervising HR Executives.
Managing recruitment requirements.
Monitoring employee records.
Reviewing HR operations.
Handling escalated employee grievances.
Coordinating payroll and compliance activities.
Implementing HR policies.
Reporting HR performance to senior management.
Example:

An organisation needs 20 new employees within 45 days.

The HR Manager allocates recruitment responsibilities, monitors progress and coordinates with department heads.

Skills Required:

Team management.
Problem-solving.
HR operations.
Communication.
Conflict management.
2.4 HR Business Partner (HRBP)
An HR Business Partner works closely with business leaders and department heads to align HR practices with departmental and organisational objectives.

Unlike a role focused mainly on administrative HR tasks, an HRBP takes a more strategic and consultative approach.

Major Responsibilities:

Understanding business requirements.
Advising department managers.
Workforce planning.
Supporting organisational changes.
Identifying talent requirements.
Improving employee performance.
Analysing workforce challenges.
Supporting employee engagement and retention.
Example:

The sales department is experiencing high employee turnover.

The HRBP works with the Sales Head to understand the reasons, analyses workforce data and recommends suitable retention strategies.

Skills Required:

Business understanding.
Analytical thinking.
Stakeholder management.
Communication.
Problem-solving.
2.5 HR Generalist
An HR Generalist handles multiple HR functions rather than specialising in just one area.

This role provides exposure to different aspects of HR operations.

Major Responsibilities:

Recruitment coordination.
Employee onboarding.
Employee documentation.
Attendance management.
Leave administration.
Payroll coordination.
Employee grievance handling.
HR policy implementation.
Performance management coordination.
Exit formalities.
Example:

In a company with 100 employees, an HR Generalist may handle recruitment, joining documentation, attendance, employee queries and exit procedures.

Skills Required:

Knowledge of multiple HR functions.
Documentation.
Excel.
Communication.
Time management.
Organisation.
2.6 HR Executive
An HR Executive is generally an operational-level professional responsible for executing HR activities.

This is one of the common entry-level positions for someone beginning an HR career.

Major Responsibilities:

Coordinating recruitment activities.
Collecting joining documents.
Maintaining employee databases.
Updating attendance records.
Coordinating leave requests.
Supporting payroll processing.
Preparing HR reports.
Coordinating employee induction.
Sending employee-related communications.
Maintaining HR documentation.
Example:

A new employee joins the organisation.

The HR Executive:

Collects the employee's documents.
Updates the employee master database.
Coordinates the joining formalities.
Shares induction information.
Updates the onboarding tracker.
Coordinates with the concerned department.
Skills Required:

MS Excel.
Documentation.
Communication.
Coordination.
Attention to detail.
Basic HR knowledge.
2.7 HR Coordinator
An HR Coordinator primarily supports the administrative and coordination aspects of HR operations.

Major Responsibilities:

Scheduling interviews.
Coordinating candidate communication.
Maintaining HR documentation.
Arranging induction sessions.
Updating employee records.
Coordinating training programmes.
Scheduling HR meetings.
Maintaining recruitment trackers.
Example:

A company has scheduled 15 interviews in one week.

The HR Coordinator contacts candidates, confirms availability, schedules interview slots and communicates with hiring managers.

2.8 Talent Acquisition Specialist
A Talent Acquisition Specialist focuses on identifying, attracting and hiring suitable candidates.

Talent Acquisition is generally broader and more long-term than simply filling immediate vacancies.

Major Responsibilities:

Candidate sourcing.
Job advertisement.
Resume screening.
Talent pipeline development.
Interview coordination.
Candidate evaluation.
Offer management.
Recruitment analytics.
Example:

An IT company needs 15 software engineers.

The Talent Acquisition Specialist identifies suitable candidates through LinkedIn, job portals, referrals and other sourcing channels.

2.9 Learning and Development (L&D) Specialist
An L&D Specialist focuses on improving employee knowledge, skills and capabilities.

Major Responsibilities:

Identifying training requirements.
Designing training programmes.
Organising workshops.
Coordinating employee development.
Evaluating training effectiveness.
Maintaining training records.
Supporting leadership development.
Example:

A hospital introduces new patient-management software.

The L&D team coordinates training sessions for employees who will use the software.

2.10 Compensation and Benefits Specialist
This professional focuses on employee compensation structures and benefits.

Major Responsibilities:

Salary structure administration.
Compensation benchmarking.
Employee benefits.
Incentive structures.
Variable pay.
Compensation policies.
Salary review coordination.
Example:

An organisation is planning annual salary revisions.

The Compensation and Benefits team analyses salary structures, internal compensation data and relevant market benchmarks.

2.11 HR Operations Specialist
HR Operations focuses on the smooth execution of routine employee-related processes.

Major Responsibilities:

Employee database management.
Employee documentation.
Attendance and leave administration.
HRMS management.
Payroll coordination.
Employee lifecycle administration.
HR reporting.
Process improvement.
Example:

An organisation has 1,000 employees across multiple locations.

The HR Operations team ensures that employee records, joining information, attendance and HR-related workflows remain accurate and updated.

TOPIC 3: UNDERSTANDING HR GENERALIST VS HR SPECIALIST
This is an important distinction for career planning.

3.1 HR Generalist
An HR Generalist handles multiple HR functions.

They have broad knowledge of different HR activities.

For example, an HR Generalist may handle:

Recruitment.
Onboarding.
Payroll coordination.
Employee relations.
Attendance.
Performance management.
Employee documentation.
3.2 HR Specialist
An HR Specialist focuses on one particular HR domain.

Examples:

Talent Acquisition Specialist.
Payroll Specialist.
Compensation and Benefits Specialist.
Learning and Development Specialist.
HR Analytics Specialist.
Employee Relations Specialist.
3.3 Comparison
Basis	HR Generalist	HR Specialist
Focus	Multiple HR functions	Specific HR function
Knowledge	Broad HR knowledge	Deep domain knowledge
Responsibilities	Diverse	Specialised
Work Exposure	Across HR activities	Concentrated in one area
Example	HR Executive	Talent Acquisition Specialist
Suitable For	People who enjoy varied responsibilities	People who prefer specialised work
3.4 Which Is Better?
Neither is universally better.

An HR Generalist role can provide broad exposure, particularly at the beginning of a career.

An HR Specialist role can help someone develop deeper expertise in a specific area.

My recommendation: At the entry level, gaining exposure to multiple HR functions is valuable. It helps someone understand which HR domain they would eventually like to specialise in.

TOPIC 4: HR DEPARTMENT STRUCTURE IN DIFFERENT ORGANISATIONS
HR departments do not have the same structure everywhere.

Their size and responsibilities depend on the organisation's workforce, industry, geographical presence and operational requirements.

4.1 HR Structure in a Small Organisation
Consider a company with 30 employees.

It may have:

One HR Manager.
One HR Executive.
The HR Manager oversees HR activities, while the HR Executive handles operational tasks.

The same HR team may manage recruitment, onboarding, attendance, payroll coordination and employee records.

4.2 HR Structure in a Medium-Sized Organisation
Consider a company with 300 employees.

Its HR structure may look like this:

                HR MANAGER
                     |
          -----------------------
          |          |          |
      HR Executive  Recruiter  HR Operations
          |          |          |
      Employee    Candidate   Employee Records
      Relations   Hiring      Attendance
      Onboarding              HRMS
                              Payroll Support
Responsibilities are divided among different employees to improve efficiency.

4.3 HR Structure in a Large Organisation
A large organisation with thousands of employees may have specialised HR divisions.

                   CHRO
                    |
                HR Director
                    |
       -----------------------------
       |          |        |        |
   HR Operations  TA      L&D      HRBP
       |          |        |        |
   HR Managers  Recruiters Trainers Business
   HR Executives          Specialists Partners
Other dedicated teams may include:

Compensation and Benefits.
Employee Relations.
HR Analytics.
HR Technology.
Organisational Development.
Workforce Planning.
Remember: These are illustrative structures. Actual reporting lines differ between organisations.

TOPIC 5: HR DEPARTMENT STRUCTURE IN A HOSPITAL
This is particularly useful because hospital operations involve several employee categories, different shifts and specialised staffing requirements.

Consider a hospital with 100 employees.

Its HR department may look like this:

             HOSPITAL ADMINISTRATOR
                       |
                   HR MANAGER
                       |
             --------------------
             |                  |
        HR Executive       HR Coordinator
             |                  |
      Employee Records     Recruitment Support
      Attendance           Interview Scheduling
      Onboarding           Joining Coordination
      Employee Queries     Training Coordination
      Payroll Support
Depending on the hospital's size, some of these responsibilities may be handled by administrative or finance teams instead.

5.1 Major Responsibilities of Hospital HR
A. Healthcare Recruitment

Recruiting:

Doctors.
Nurses.
Biomedical Engineers.
Lab Technicians.
Pharmacists.
Hospital Administrators.
Support Staff.
HR must coordinate qualification verification, experience checks and relevant professional credentials according to the role and applicable requirements.

B. Shift and Roster Coordination

Hospitals operate across different shifts.

HR coordinates with department heads regarding:

Employee availability.
Shift schedules.
Weekly offs.
Leave requests.
Overtime records.
Attendance discrepancies.
C. Employee Documentation

Maintaining relevant employee records, such as:

Educational certificates.
Identity documents.
Employment details.
Appointment letters.
Relevant professional registrations.
Training records.
Access to sensitive documents must be appropriately restricted.

D. Employee Training

Coordinating:

Workplace safety training.
Infection prevention training.
Emergency response training.
Equipment-related training.
Employee orientation.
E. Employee Welfare

Supporting:

Employee grievances.
Workplace safety.
Employee engagement.
Staff welfare initiatives.
Workplace communication.
5.2 Practical Example
A hospital requires:

Position	Required Employees
Nurses	5
Biomedical Engineers	2
Lab Technicians	3
Hospital Administrators	2
Total	12
The HR department must:

Confirm staffing requirements with department heads.
Prepare job descriptions.
Publish job advertisements.
Source suitable candidates.
Screen applications.
Coordinate interviews.
Process selected candidates.
Verify relevant documents and credentials.
Issue employment documentation.
Coordinate onboarding.
This is an example of HR supporting the operational requirements of a healthcare organisation.

TOPIC 6: SKILLS REQUIRED TO WORK IN HR
An HR professional requires a combination of technical, interpersonal and organisational skills.

6.1 Communication Skills
HR professionals communicate with:

Employees.
Managers.
Candidates.
Recruiters.
Senior management.
External service providers.
They must be able to communicate clearly through emails, phone calls, meetings and professional conversations.

Example: Explaining joining documentation requirements to a newly selected candidate.

6.2 Interpersonal Skills
Interpersonal skills refer to the ability to interact effectively with other people.

They include:

Empathy.
Active listening.
Relationship building.
Cooperation.
Respectful communication.
Example: Listening carefully to an employee who raises a workplace concern.

6.3 Problem-Solving Skills
HR professionals frequently encounter unexpected problems.

Examples:

Candidates withdrawing from recruitment.
Payroll discrepancies.
Employee grievances.
Attendance errors.
Workplace conflicts.
They must understand the situation, identify possible causes and coordinate appropriate solutions.

6.4 Organisational Skills
HR professionals handle multiple responsibilities simultaneously.

They need to:

Prioritise tasks.
Maintain deadlines.
Organise documents.
Track employee information.
Coordinate multiple activities.
6.5 Technical Skills
Important technical skills include:

Skill	HR Application
MS Excel	Employee databases and HR analytics
MS Word	HR documentation
PowerPoint	HR presentations
Email Communication	Employee and candidate communication
HRMS	Employee information management
ATS	Recruitment management
Google Sheets	HR trackers
Power BI	HR dashboards and analytics
6.6 Ethical and Confidentiality Skills
HR professionals frequently handle sensitive information.

Examples:

Employee salaries.
Personal information.
Performance evaluations.
Grievances.
Disciplinary records.
Recruitment decisions.
They must maintain confidentiality and share information only with authorised individuals for legitimate work-related purposes.

TOPIC 7: WHAT DOES AN HR EXECUTIVE ACTUALLY DO IN A DAY?
This section is particularly important because it connects theoretical knowledge to a real job.

Consider an HR Executive working in a medium-sized organisation.

Sample Daily Schedule
Time	Activity
9:30 AM	Check emails and pending HR tasks
10:00 AM	Update employee attendance records
10:30 AM	Coordinate scheduled interviews
11:30 AM	Review candidate applications
12:30 PM	Update recruitment tracker
1:00 PM	Lunch
2:00 PM	Collect documents from new joiners
3:00 PM	Update employee master database
4:00 PM	Coordinate with finance regarding payroll queries
4:30 PM	Respond to employee queries
5:00 PM	Prepare HR reports
5:30 PM	Review pending tasks and plan the next day
This is only an illustrative schedule. Actual responsibilities and working hours vary between organisations.

Important Understanding
An HR Executive does not spend the entire day interviewing people or conducting employee engagement activities.

A considerable amount of HR work involves:

Documentation.
Coordination.
Data entry.
Follow-ups.
Reporting.
Employee communication.
Process management.
Someone entering HR must be comfortable with administrative responsibilities as well as people-oriented work.
