PART 1: COMPREHENSIVE REVISION NOTES
TOPIC 1: FUNDAMENTALS OF HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT
1.1 Definition of HRM
Human Resource Management is the process of managing an organisation's employees through recruitment, selection, training, compensation, performance management, employee engagement and other workforce-related activities.

The primary objective is to ensure that employees contribute effectively towards organisational goals while receiving appropriate support and opportunities for development.

1.2 Four Major Objectives of HRM
Objective	Explanation	Example
Organisational	Supporting business objectives	Hiring employees for expansion
Functional	Ensuring HR activities operate effectively	Maintaining recruitment processes
Personal	Supporting employee development	Providing training
Societal	Maintaining responsible employment practices	Promoting workplace fairness
1.3 Major Functions of HRM
Managerial Functions

Planning.
Organising.
Directing.
Controlling.
Operative Functions

Recruitment and selection.
Training and development.
Compensation management.
Employee integration.
Employee maintenance.
Separation management.
1.4 Evolution of HRM
Stage	Primary Focus
Personnel Management	Employee administration and record keeping
Human Resource Management	Employee development and management
Strategic HRM	Aligning workforce strategy with business objectives
Revision Question: Why is HR considered a strategic function rather than simply an administrative department?

Expected answer: Because workforce decisions influence organisational productivity, expansion, employee retention, costs and long-term business performance.

TOPIC 2: HR DEPARTMENT STRUCTURE AND RESPONSIBILITIES
2.1 HR Hierarchy
A typical HR hierarchy:

Chief Human Resources Officer.
HR Director.
HR Manager.
HR Business Partner.
HR Executive / HR Generalist.
HR Coordinator.
HR Assistant.
The exact hierarchy differs depending on organisational size.

2.2 Major HR Specialisations
HR Function	Main Responsibility
Talent Acquisition	Recruitment and selection
HR Operations	Employee documentation and administration
Payroll	Salary processing and payroll coordination
Learning & Development	Employee training
Employee Relations	Grievances and workplace relationships
Compensation & Benefits	Salary structures and employee benefits
HR Analytics	Workforce data analysis
2.3 HR Generalist vs HR Specialist
An HR Generalist handles multiple HR functions.

An HR Specialist focuses on one particular area, such as recruitment, payroll or learning and development.

Example:

An HR Generalist may coordinate recruitment, onboarding, attendance and employee documentation.

A Talent Acquisition Specialist primarily focuses on sourcing, screening, interviewing coordination and hiring.

TOPIC 3: EMPLOYEE LIFECYCLE MANAGEMENT
The employee lifecycle describes the different stages of an employee's relationship with an organisation.

Seven Stages
Attraction.
Recruitment and Selection.
Onboarding.
Development.
Engagement and Retention.
Performance Management.
Separation.
Important Concepts
Concept	Meaning
Recruitment	Attracting potential candidates
Selection	Choosing suitable candidates
Onboarding	Integrating new employees into the organisation
Training	Improving job-related skills
Employee Engagement	Building employee involvement and commitment
Retention	Keeping employees within the organisation
Attrition	Employees leaving an organisation
Performance Management	Monitoring and improving employee performance
Exit Interview	Gathering feedback from departing employees
Important Distinction
Recruitment and selection are not the same.

Recruitment attracts candidates.

Selection evaluates candidates and identifies the most suitable person for the role.

TOPIC 4: HR POLICIES AND ORGANISATIONAL CULTURE
4.1 What is an HR Policy?
An HR policy is a formal organisational guideline that establishes how employee-related matters should be handled.

Important HR Policies
Attendance Policy.
Leave Policy.
Recruitment Policy.
Code of Conduct.
Performance Management Policy.
Employee Grievance Policy.
Confidentiality Policy.
Disciplinary Policy.
Anti-Harassment Policy.
Data Protection Policy.
4.2 Policy vs Procedure vs Rule vs Guideline
Term	Meaning
Policy	Establishes organisational direction
Procedure	Explains how an activity is performed
Rule	Specifies a requirement or restriction
Guideline	Provides recommended practices
4.3 Organisational Culture
Organisational culture refers to the shared values, beliefs, attitudes and behavioural expectations within an organisation.

Four commonly discussed culture types:

Culture	Characteristics
Clan	Teamwork and collaboration
Adhocracy	Innovation and experimentation
Market	Competition and results
Hierarchy	Structure and formal processes
4.4 HR Ethics
Important ethical responsibilities include:

Confidentiality.
Fairness.
Transparency.
Professional behaviour.
Avoiding discrimination.
Responsible handling of employee information.
Fair treatment during disciplinary procedures.
TOPIC 5: STRATEGIC HRM AND WORKFORCE PLANNING
5.1 Strategic HRM
Strategic HRM aligns workforce management with long-term organisational objectives.

Example:

A hospital planning to expand its ICU facilities must plan for additional nurses, technicians and support employees.

5.2 Workforce Planning Process
Understand organisational objectives.
Analyse existing employees.
Forecast future workforce demand.
Forecast future workforce supply.
Identify workforce gaps.
Develop a manpower plan.
Implement and monitor the plan.
5.3 Workforce Gap Formula
Workforce Gap = Workforce Demand − Workforce Supply.

Example:

Required Employees = 50.

Available Employees = 42.

Workforce Gap = 8 employees.

5.4 Important HR Metrics
Metric	Formula
Attrition Rate	Employees who left ÷ Average Headcount × 100
Retention Rate	Employees retained from opening workforce ÷ Opening Headcount × 100
Absenteeism Rate	Absent Workdays ÷ Scheduled Workdays × 100
Cost per Hire	Recruitment Costs ÷ Number of Hires
Offer Acceptance Rate	Accepted Offers ÷ Offers Extended × 100
Training Completion Rate	Employees Completing Training ÷ Employees Assigned Training × 100
Remember: A metric is only useful when HR understands what it indicates and investigates the underlying reasons.

TOPIC 6: HEALTHCARE HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT
Healthcare HR manages employees working in hospitals, diagnostic centres, clinics and other healthcare organisations.

Categories of Healthcare Employees
Category	Examples
Clinical	Doctors, Nurses, Surgeons
Technical	Biomedical Engineers, Lab Technicians
Administrative	HR Executives, Receptionists
Support	Housekeeping, Security, Maintenance
Major Healthcare HR Responsibilities
Recruitment and credential verification.
Attendance and leave management.
Shift scheduling coordination.
Employee onboarding.
Training coordination.
Employee documentation.
Performance management coordination.
Employee relations.
Workplace safety coordination.
Important Distinction
HR is responsible for coordinating recruitment and employee processes.

The concerned technical or clinical department evaluates professional competence.

For example, HR may recruit a Biomedical Engineer, but the Biomedical Department evaluates the candidate's knowledge of medical equipment.
