Today's Objective
Today focuses on understanding how Human Resource Management operates in hospitals and healthcare organisations.

Healthcare HR is different from HR in a conventional corporate office because hospitals operate continuously, require specialised professionals, manage shift-based staffing and have additional patient-safety and confidentiality considerations.

By the end of today, she should understand how a hospital HR department functions and how HR coordinates with medical, technical and administrative departments.

TOPIC 1: INTRODUCTION TO HEALTHCARE HR MANAGEMENT
1.1 What is Healthcare HR Management?
Healthcare Human Resource Management refers to the process of managing employees working in hospitals, clinics, diagnostic centres, laboratories and other healthcare organisations.

It involves planning, recruiting, training, managing, evaluating and retaining healthcare employees.

Unlike many conventional organisations, hospitals require different categories of professionals working together to deliver healthcare services.

These include:

Doctors and surgeons.
Nurses.
Biomedical Engineers.
Laboratory Technicians.
Pharmacists.
Radiology Technicians.
Hospital Administrators.
Receptionists.
Housekeeping Staff.
Security Personnel.
Finance and HR Professionals.
Healthcare HR ensures that the organisation has sufficient qualified personnel to support its operations.

1.2 Why is HR Important in Hospitals?
1. Patient Care Continuity

Hospitals must maintain adequate staffing to support continuous patient care.

For example, a hospital operating 24 hours a day needs appropriate staffing arrangements for day, evening and night shifts.

2. Recruitment of Qualified Professionals

Healthcare positions often require specific educational qualifications, registrations, certifications or technical competencies.

HR must coordinate with department heads to identify suitable candidates and verify relevant credentials.

3. Employee Training

Healthcare employees may require training in:

Patient safety.
Infection prevention.
Emergency procedures.
Equipment handling.
Workplace safety.
Hospital protocols.
4. Shift and Attendance Management

Hospitals operate across different shifts. HR must coordinate attendance, leave, overtime and shift allocation.

5. Employee Retention

Healthcare professionals may experience demanding workloads and irregular schedules.

HR helps improve employee satisfaction, engagement and retention.

6. Regulatory and Policy Compliance

Healthcare organisations must follow applicable employment requirements and relevant healthcare workplace policies.

7. Employee Safety and Well-being

Hospitals need to protect employees from occupational risks, workplace harassment and unsafe working conditions.

TOPIC 2: STRUCTURE OF A HOSPITAL HR DEPARTMENT
A hospital's HR department may be small or large depending on the organisation's size and operational complexity.

A large hospital may have specialised HR teams, whereas a smaller hospital may have only an HR Manager and a few HR Executives.

2.1 Typical Hospital HR Hierarchy
A simplified structure:

↓

Specialised HR Functions:

Talent Acquisition and Recruitment.
HR Operations and Administration.

HR Executives / Coordinators / Assistants

2.2 Important Hospital HR Roles
Designation	Main Responsibilities
HR Head	Oversees overall HR strategy and workforce management
HR Manager	Manages HR operations, policies and employee-related decisions
Talent Acquisition Specialist	Recruits doctors, nurses, technicians and administrative staff
HR Executive	Handles recruitment coordination, documentation and employee support
HR Operations Executive	Maintains employee records and HR processes
Payroll Executive	Coordinates salary processing, deductions and payroll records
Training Coordinator	Organises employee training programmes
Employee Relations Executive	Handles employee concerns, grievances and workplace matters
HR Coordinator	Supports scheduling, documentation and communication
2.3 How HR Coordinates with Other Hospital Departments
HR does not independently decide every operational requirement.

It works closely with department heads.

Department	HR Coordination
Nursing	Nurse recruitment, shift-related staffing, attendance
Medical	Doctor recruitment, credential verification, contracts
Biomedical	Biomedical Engineer recruitment and technical training
Laboratory	Technician staffing and qualification verification
Pharmacy	Pharmacist recruitment and employee records
Administration	Receptionist and administrative staffing
Finance	Payroll information, salary coordination and budgeting
Housekeeping	Staffing, attendance and training
Practical Understanding:

Suppose the Biomedical Department requires two additional Biomedical Engineers.

The department head informs HR about the requirement.

HR coordinates the recruitment process, prepares or finalises the job description with the department, sources candidates, schedules interviews and coordinates the offer and joining process.

The technical department evaluates whether the candidate has the necessary technical knowledge.

HR and the department head therefore have different but complementary responsibilities.

Recruiting a Receptionist requires greater emphasis on communication, administrative skills and patient interaction.

TOPIC 4: HEALTHCARE RECRUITMENT AND CREDENTIAL VERIFICATION
Healthcare recruitment requires particular attention to qualifications and professional suitability.

4.1 Recruitment Process in Hospitals
A typical healthcare recruitment process involves:

Step 1: Manpower Requirement

The department head identifies a vacancy.

Example: The ICU department requires three additional nurses.

Step 2: Job Description Preparation

HR coordinates with the department to establish the responsibilities and requirements.

Step 3: Candidate Sourcing

Candidates may be sourced through:

Job portals.
Hospital career pages.
Employee referrals.
Recruitment agencies.
Campus placements.
Professional networks.
Step 4: Resume Screening

HR checks whether the candidate's educational qualifications and experience match the job requirements.

Step 5: Interview Coordination

HR schedules interviews with the relevant department.

Step 6: Technical Evaluation

The concerned department assesses technical competence.

Step 7: Credential and Background Verification

HR coordinates the verification of relevant documents.

Depending on the position, this may include:

Educational certificates.
Professional registrations.
Experience certificates.
Identity documents.
References.
Background verification.
Relevant licences or certifications.
Step 8: Selection and Offer

The selected candidate receives an employment offer.

Step 9: Joining and Onboarding

HR completes joining formalities and coordinates induction.

4.2 Why is Credential Verification Important?
Credential verification helps ensure that employees possess the qualifications and professional credentials required for their roles.

Example:

A hospital recruiting a registered nurse must verify relevant nursing qualifications and applicable professional registration.

Similarly, a hospital recruiting a Biomedical Engineer may verify the candidate's engineering degree and relevant technical experience.

Important distinction: HR coordinates and records verification. Technical or clinical departments must assess role-specific competence where required.

TOPIC 5: SHIFT MANAGEMENT IN HOSPITALS
One of the major differences between hospital HR and conventional office HR is shift management.

Hospitals may operate 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

Therefore, HR must coordinate staffing across different shifts.

5.1 What is Shift Management?
Shift management refers to organising employee working hours to ensure that operational requirements are fulfilled.

A hospital may use the following illustrative shift structure:

Shift	Working Hours
Morning Shift	7 AM – 3 PM
Evening Shift	3 PM – 11 PM
Night Shift	11 PM – 7 AM
These are examples, not universal hospital timings.

5.2 Why is Shift Management Important?
Ensures operational continuity.
Maintains adequate staffing.
Distributes workloads.
Helps manage employee availability.
Supports patient-care operations.
Reduces scheduling conflicts.
5.3 Important Factors in Shift Scheduling
1. Employee Availability

HR must consider employee availability, approved leave and existing schedules.

2. Staffing Requirements

Different departments require different staffing levels.

For example, ICU staffing requirements cannot be treated the same as routine administrative office staffing.

3. Employee Skills

Employees assigned to shifts must have the appropriate qualifications and competencies.

4. Workload Distribution

HR should coordinate with department heads to prevent unfair or excessive workload distribution.

5. Weekly Offs and Leave

Weekly offs and approved leave must be incorporated into schedules.

6. Applicable Working-Hour Requirements

Shift schedules must comply with applicable employment laws, rules and organisational policies.

5.4 Practical Shift Scheduling Example
A hospital requires six nurses across three shifts.

Assume the following minimum staffing requirement:

Shift	Required Nurses
Morning	3
Evening	2
Night	1
Total	6
HR and the Nursing Supervisor must prepare a roster that maintains coverage while considering weekly offs, leave and other applicable working-hour requirements.

Important: Six nurses may not be sufficient to maintain these positions every day because employees also require weekly rest and leave. Actual staffing must account for the complete roster cycle.

5.5 What is a Duty Roster?
A duty roster is a schedule showing which employees are assigned to specific shifts or duties.

It typically includes:

Employee name.
Employee ID.
Department.
Shift timing.
Weekly off.
Leave status.
Assigned responsibilities.
A duty roster helps HR and department managers monitor employee deployment.

TOPIC 6: ATTENDANCE AND LEAVE MANAGEMENT IN HOSPITALS
Attendance management is particularly important in healthcare because an employee's absence can affect departmental operations.

6.1 Common Attendance Systems
Hospitals may use:

Biometric attendance.
RFID attendance cards.
Facial recognition systems.
Attendance registers.
HRMS-based attendance tracking.
6.2 Responsibilities of HR
HR may be responsible for:

Maintaining attendance records.
Monitoring late arrivals.
Recording absences.
Processing leave requests.
Coordinating attendance corrections.
Sharing attendance information with payroll.
Preparing attendance reports.
6.3 Common Leave Categories
Leave Type	Meaning
Casual Leave	Leave for personal or unforeseen requirements
Sick Leave	Leave due to illness
Earned Leave	Leave accumulated according to applicable policy
Maternity Leave	Leave associated with maternity
Paternity Leave	Leave provided under applicable organisational policy or law
Leave Without Pay	Approved absence without salary for the relevant period
Compensatory Off	Time off provided under an applicable policy for eligible additional work
Important: Leave eligibility and entitlements depend on applicable laws, employment arrangements and organisational policies. HR must verify the rules applicable to the establishment rather than assuming every hospital follows identical leave provisions.

6.4 Practical Scenario
A nurse assigned to the night shift informs HR that she is unwell and cannot report to work.

HR should:

Record the leave request.
Inform the Nursing Supervisor.
Check available staffing.
Coordinate a replacement if necessary.
Update the attendance system.
Maintain appropriate documentation.
The objective is to manage employee absence without compromising essential hospital operations.

TOPIC 7: EMPLOYEE TRAINING IN HEALTHCARE
Healthcare employees require continuous training because their work involves patient safety, technical equipment and established hospital procedures.

7.1 What is Employee Training?
Employee training is a structured process through which employees acquire knowledge and skills required to perform their current jobs effectively.

7.2 Common Healthcare Training Programmes
Training Programme	Target Employees
Infection Prevention	Clinical and support staff
Fire Safety	All employees
Emergency Response	Relevant clinical and support teams
Patient Handling	Nursing and support staff
Equipment Handling	Biomedical and clinical staff
Data Confidentiality	Employees handling patient information
Workplace Safety	Employees across departments
Communication Skills	Front-office and patient-facing staff
7.3 Training Needs Analysis (TNA)
Training Needs Analysis is the process of identifying the difference between employees' existing capabilities and the skills required for their jobs.

Example:

A hospital introduces a new patient-monitoring system.

Existing employees may not know how to operate it.

HR coordinates with the Biomedical Department to identify employees requiring training and arrange appropriate sessions.

7.4 Basic Training Process
Identify the training requirement.
Determine the employees who need training.
Define learning objectives.
Select a trainer.
Schedule the programme.
Conduct training.
Evaluate learning.
Record completion.
Monitor workplace application.
7.5 How Can HR Measure Training?
HR may monitor:

Number of employees trained.
Training completion rate.
Assessment scores.
Employee feedback.
Post-training performance.
Reduction in identified skill gaps.
TOPIC 8: PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT IN HOSPITALS
Performance management is the process of setting expectations, monitoring employee performance, providing feedback and supporting improvement.

8.1 Why is Performance Management Important?
It helps organisations:

Evaluate employee performance.
Identify skill gaps.
Improve productivity.
Recognise good performance.
Identify training requirements.
Support employee development.
8.2 Examples of Performance Indicators
Different departments require different performance indicators.

Department	Illustrative Performance Indicators
Nursing	Documentation quality, completion of assigned duties, protocol adherence
Biomedical	Preventive maintenance completion, equipment downtime, maintenance documentation
HR	Recruitment turnaround time, documentation accuracy, onboarding completion
Reception	Patient registration accuracy, communication, service responsiveness
Housekeeping	Cleaning schedule adherence, inspection results, hygiene protocol compliance
These indicators must be interpreted according to role requirements and should not encourage unsafe shortcuts.

8.3 What are KPIs?
Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) are measurable indicators used to evaluate progress towards specific performance objectives.

Example:

A recruitment team's objective is to fill approved vacancies efficiently.

Possible KPI:

Average Time to Fill.

A Biomedical Department's objective is to maintain equipment availability.

Possible KPI:

Preventive Maintenance Completion Rate.

Important: HR may coordinate the performance management process, but department heads and technical supervisors are usually responsible for evaluating role-specific performance.

Hospitals present occupational risks that may differ from those in conventional office environments.

Examples include:

Biological exposure.
Chemical exposure.
Needle-stick injuries.
Radiation-related risks in relevant departments.
Electrical and equipment-related hazards.
Physical strain.

9.1 HR's Role in Employee Safety
HR may coordinate:

Employee safety training.
Workplace policies.
Incident documentation.
Occupational health coordination.
Employee awareness programmes.
Grievance reporting mechanisms.
Coordination with safety and infection-control departments.
HR does not replace the responsibilities of qualified safety, medical or infection-control professionals.

9.2 Employee Well-being
Employee well-being refers to the physical, mental and workplace-related welfare of employees.

Possible initiatives:

Employee assistance programmes.
Health check-up coordination.
Fair shift scheduling.
Grievance mechanisms.
Recognition programmes.
Stress-management initiatives.
Employee feedback sessions.
9.3 Workplace Harassment and Professional Conduct
Healthcare organisations must maintain professional and respectful workplaces.

HR may coordinate complaint-handling processes, documentation and appropriate internal mechanisms under applicable policies and law.

For sexual harassment complaints in India, the POSH framework is relevant. The organisation must follow the applicable legal requirements and prescribed procedures.

10.3 Confidentiality in Healthcare HR
Employee records may contain sensitive personal information.

Patient-related information may also be encountered during hospital HR operations.

HR must follow appropriate access controls and confidentiality requirements.

For example, an HR Executive should not share an employee's salary details with unauthorised colleagues.

Similarly, patient records should not be accessed or shared merely because an employee works in the hospital.
